----------- Overall impression -----------
The paper presents an up-to-date taxonomic analysis of ISO/IEC 27001:2022, yielding a SKOS taxonomy representing the standard. The analysis is well-structured, reproducible, and provides artifacts of practical value, including an RDF model of the standard, SPARQL queries, scripts, and visualizations.
----------- Major strong points -----------
- Clear research gaps have been identified via literature review.
- Systematic, reproducible analysis.
- Useful outcomes, publicly available on GitHub.
- Well-written
----------- Major weak points -----------
- Minor justification gaps, such as why Apache Jena ARQ was used in combination with Python rdflib, if the latter can also run SPARQL queries (although slower).
- "CQs are standard in semantic modeling but rarely applied to security standards". Where did this come from? What does support this claim?
- No major conclusion was drawn from the analysis. This is probably the weakest point of the paper. At least, the study observes that the resulting taxonomy reveals a high sparsity of the encoded elements (a lack of interconnection), but it is unclear whether this is a feature of the standard or the taxonomic model of it.
